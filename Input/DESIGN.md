**# STARLIGHT YARNOVATE**

**## VISUAL DESIGN SYSTEM v1.0**



**### 1. Design Philosophy**

**\*\*Creative warmth × Editorial boldness × Tactile craftsmanship × Modern simplicity\*\***



**The website should feel like a beautifully curated textile studio translated into a modern digital experience.**



**Use pastelism as the atmosphere, bold typography as the structure, and authentic crochet photography as the personality.**



**Do not make the website look like a generic pastel lifestyle template. The design must have a distinct composition, intentional contrasts, and a strong visual identity.**



**### 2. Colour Palette**



**\*\*Primary colours\*\***

**- Warm Ivory: `#FAF7F0` — main page background.**

**- Soft Blush: `#EED8D4` — selected background panels.**

**- Muted Sage: `#BECBBC` — secondary accents.**

**- Dusty Lavender: `#DCD5E8` — occasional section variation.**

**- Deep Cocoa: `#402B27` — primary text and strong dark sections.**

**- Terracotta: `#A9573F` — primary CTA and selective emphasis.**



**\*\*Supporting colours\*\***

**- Oatmeal: `#E5D8C5` — subtle backgrounds and borders.**

**- Pure White: `#FFFFFF` — image surrounds and clean panels.**

**- Soft Charcoal: `#292725` — high-legibility text.**



**\*\*Usage rules\*\***

**- Ivory should dominate the overall canvas.**

**- Use blush, sage, lavender, and oatmeal selectively rather than giving every section a different pastel colour.**

**- Use Deep Cocoa or Soft Charcoal for important text.**

**- Reserve Terracotta for primary actions, small emphasis blocks, and selected visual details.**

**- Keep text/background contrast strong. Never sacrifice readability for a soft aesthetic.**

**- Avoid rainbow pastels, neon colours, and excessive gradients.**



**### 3. Typography**



**Use a maximum of two typeface families.**



**\*\*Display / editorial headings\*\***

**- Preferred: `DM Serif Display`**

**- Alternative: `Playfair Display`**

**- Weight: Regular or bold where supported; create visual strength through size, line breaks, and selective emphasis.**

**- Character: expressive, editorial, elegant, and recognisable.**



**\*\*Body / navigation / interface\*\***

**- Preferred: `DM Sans`**

**- Alternative: `Manrope`**

**- Weight: 400 for body text; 500–600 for navigation and controls; 650–700 for important interface headings when supported.**

**- Character: clear, modern, confident, and readable.**



**Use reliable fallbacks:**

**`Georgia, serif` for display text and `Arial, sans-serif` for interface text.**



**Load fonts responsibly and ensure the page remains readable if external font loading fails.**



**\*\*Type scale\*\***

**- Hero heading: `clamp(3.2rem, 6.5vw, 6.5rem)`**

**- Page heading: `clamp(2.7rem, 5vw, 4.8rem)`**

**- Section heading: `clamp(2rem, 3.5vw, 3.2rem)`**

**- Card / subsection heading: `1.25–1.65rem`**

**- Body: `1–1.125rem`**

**- Supporting text: `0.875–0.95rem`**

**- Navigation: `0.8125–0.9375rem`**



**Use generous heading line-height control, generally around 0.95–1.12 for large display text and 1.5–1.75 for body copy.**



**\*\*Typography rules\*\***

**- Make the hero headline bold in presence, not merely large.**

**- Use intentional line breaks to create composition.**

**- Use occasional terracotta highlighting or a coloured text panel.**

**- Avoid thin uppercase headings, excessive letter spacing, script fonts for essential content, and too many font weights.**

**- Body copy must remain comfortably readable on mobile.**



**### 4. Layout and Composition**



**\*\*Overall structure\*\***

**- Use a consistent content grid with a maximum content width around 1440px.**

**- Desktop page gutters: approximately 5–7vw.**

**- Tablet gutters: approximately 28–36px.**

**- Mobile gutters: approximately 20–24px.**

**- Use generous vertical spacing and consistent alignment.**



**\*\*Spacing scale\*\***

**- `4px`, `8px`, `12px`, `16px`, `24px`, `32px`, `48px`, `64px`, `88px`, `112px`, `144px`.**



**Use smaller spacing for related controls and larger spacing to distinguish major sections.**



**\*\*Shape language\*\***

**- Predominantly clean edges and restrained corner radii.**

**- Small-to-medium radii for images and selected panels.**

**- Use one or two deliberate oversized rounded shapes only if they strengthen the composition.**

**- Do not round every card, button, image, and section identically.**



**### 5. Homepage Hero — Signature Composition**



**The hero is the most important visual element.**



**\*\*Desktop\*\***

**- Build a spacious, asymmetrical two-part composition.**

**- One side contains a large editorial headline, concise supporting text, and clear CTA buttons.**

**- The other side features a large, striking photograph of real crochet work, yarn texture, hands crafting, or a finished handmade piece.**

**- Let the photograph have a strong silhouette and occupy substantial visual space.**

**- Use selective overlapping, a subtle colour field, or a small editorial label only when it improves the composition.**

**- Keep the headline and image balanced; neither should feel like an afterthought.**

**- Include a compact navigation system with clear hierarchy.**



**\*\*Headline direction\*\***

**Use a confident, concise line about learning crochet and creating by hand. Final wording should reflect the owner's approved brand voice.**



**\*\*Mobile\*\***

**- Stack content in a deliberate order.**

**- Keep the headline large but prevent awkward orphan words.**

**- Place the primary CTA where it is easy to reach.**

**- Preserve a strong image rather than shrinking it into an insignificant thumbnail.**

**- Do not hide navigation or contact details behind confusing interactions.**



**\*\*Reference interpretation\*\***

**Retain the reference's oversized crochet image, editorial typography, open whitespace, and refined simplicity. Improve readability, visual boldness, and mobile adaptation. Do not duplicate the original website's logo, wording, or exact composition.**



**### 6. Photography and Image Art Direction**



**\*\*Priority:\*\* Authentic crochet work and genuine teaching imagery.**



**Preferred imagery:**

**- Close-ups of crochet stitches and yarn texture.**

**- Hands working with a crochet hook.**

**- Finished handmade pieces photographed in natural light.**

**- Yarn, hooks, textile details, and a calm creative workspace.**

**- Real instructor photographs when provided.**



**\*\*Image treatment\*\***

**- Warm natural lighting.**

**- Realistic colour and texture.**

**- Tactile detail without artificial oversharpening.**

**- Simple backgrounds that separate the crochet piece from its surroundings.**

**- A consistent photographic mood across the website.**



**\*\*Avoid\*\***

**- Obvious AI-generated hands, hooks, yarn, and stitch patterns.**

**- Repetitive stock photos.**

**- Unrelated lifestyle imagery.**

**- Overly polished 3D illustrations and plastic-looking textures.**

**- Fake photographs of students or classes.**

**- Generic photos that do not communicate crochet.**



**If authentic photos are limited, use a small, coherent collection of relevant imagery rather than filling every section with stock photos.**



**### 7. Navigation and Components**



**\*\*Navigation\*\***

**- Brand identity on the left or centre, depending on the composition.**

**- Links: Home, About, Classes, Gallery, Contact.**

**- A clear enquiry action.**

**- Clean typography, comfortable spacing, and a simple mobile menu.**



**\*\*Primary button\*\***

**- Terracotta background.**

**- Light text with strong contrast.**

**- Medium-weight sans-serif label.**

**- Comfortable padding and a restrained radius.**

**- Clear hover, focus, and active states.**



**\*\*Secondary button\*\***

**- Transparent or ivory background.**

**- Cocoa text.**

**- Thin, subtle border or understated text-link treatment.**



**\*\*Image galleries\*\***

**- Use varied image proportions and a deliberate editorial grid.**

**- Avoid making every image the same size unless the photography benefits from uniformity.**

**- Use consistent gutters and predictable interaction behaviour.**



**\*\*Section labels\*\***

**- Small uppercase sans-serif text can be used sparingly for categories or section introductions.**

**- Keep them readable and avoid excessive tracking.**



**\*\*Icons\*\***

**- Use simple, consistent line icons only when needed.**

**- Prefer clear text labels for important contact actions.**

**- No emoji-based interface design.**



**### 8. Motion and Interaction**



**- Use subtle transitions for hover, focus, navigation, and image enlargement.**

**- Keep durations generally around 150–300ms.**

**- Optional restrained reveal effects may be used if they do not delay access to content.**

**- No distracting parallax, endless floating animations, cursor effects, or complex scroll-triggered sequences.**

**- Respect reduced-motion preferences.**

**- All controls must work on touchscreens and keyboards.**



**### 9. Anti-AI-Slop Rules**



**These rules are mandatory:**



**1. Do not use a generic hero with a gradient background and a floating card.**

**2. Do not repeat the same three-card layout in every section.**

**3. Do not use excessive pills, badges, glass panels, shadows, and rounded rectangles.**

**4. Do not use randomly generated abstract decorations to fill empty space.**

**5. Do not choose fonts simply because they are trendy.**

**6. Do not reduce every section to a centred heading followed by three identical cards.**

**7. Do not add fake statistics, testimonials, logos, or invented credibility signals.**

**8. Do not create arbitrary colour changes between adjacent sections.**

**9. Do not over-animate basic content.**

**10. Do not treat a visually complete page as a finished design without checking actual browser rendering.**



**\*\*Positive requirement:\*\* Every major section must have a clear visual purpose and a deliberate composition. Introduce variety through photography, alignment, scale, and whitespace—not through a collection of unrelated effects.**



**### 10. Responsive and Accessibility Standards**



**- Mobile-first responsive implementation.**

**- No horizontal overflow at common screen widths.**

**- Comfortable touch targets, preferably around 44 × 44px.**

**- Visible keyboard focus states.**

**- Semantic heading hierarchy.**

**- Descriptive alt text for meaningful images.**

**- Strong contrast for text and controls.**

**- Functional links and accessible navigation.**

**- Respect reduced-motion settings.**



**### 11. Design Acceptance Checklist**



**Before declaring the website complete, verify:**



**- \[ ] The first viewport looks custom-designed and memorable.**

**- \[ ] Hero typography is bold, clear, and readable.**

**- \[ ] Crochet imagery is authentic-looking and visually prominent.**

**- \[ ] Pastel colours create atmosphere without reducing contrast.**

**- \[ ] Layouts vary intentionally between sections and pages.**

**- \[ ] No generic template patterns dominate the website.**

**- \[ ] Fonts, spacing, buttons, and imagery feel like one coherent identity.**

**- \[ ] Mobile layouts preserve the design's character.**

**- \[ ] No unsupported business claims or pricing appear.**

**- \[ ] The final browser rendering has been visually reviewed and corrected.**



**\*\*Design principle:\*\* Pastel in atmosphere. Bold in typography. Tactile in imagery. Precise in layout. Restrained in effects. Distinctive in composition.**
